# Stage 3: our own far terrain (LOD) — design

Requirement: docs/GOAL.md stage 3 and amendments ("extremely optimized Distant Horizons/Voxy equivalent whose distant terrain
is visually indistinguishable", zoom at full quality, render-distance model "real chunks at RD 12–16, LOD beyond", minimum
standard 200 FPS with shaders + RT + 256 chunks, multiplayer server-invisible, Fabric-compatible). Grounded in
docs/stage3/ZOOM.md (zoom requirements on stage 3, D1–D10), docs/stage4/OWN-SHADERS-RT.md (§5.4, §6.14, §10.3: LOD clusters,
voxel world, far ray fields), results/ROUND12.md + CONTRACT 16.R12-A (GPU cluster culling), results/STAGE2-R1.md (chunk
pipeline costs) and CONTRACT 16.FAB (no vanilla signature changes).

Status tags: **[M1]** built in stage 3 round 1 (`-Dmc.lod=true`, default off; results/STAGE3-R1.md), **[next]** designed, not
built. Numbers are tagged *(measured)*, *(model)* or *(estimate)* as in ZOOM.md.

## 1. Render-distance model and targets

- Real chunks: RD 12–16 (GOAL amendment 2026-10-06). LOD: from the real edge to `mc.lod.radius` (64 / 128 / 256 chunks).
- Bars: FPS with LOD >= 70 % of the same view without LOD (floor, not target); visually indistinguishable from real terrain at
  that distance, unzoomed and zoomed (spyglass 10x, zoom key up to 50x); 200 FPS minimum with stage-4 shaders + stage-5 RT at
  256 chunks later.
- What the bars imply (ZOOM.md §2): at FOV 70 on the 1964-row panel K = 1402 px; one block is 1 px at 1402 blocks (88
  chunks), so level 0 (block geometry) is needed to ~44–88 chunks unzoomed (tau 1–2) and across the whole 256-chunk disc
  under the spyglass (K = 16,056). Coarse levels only exist where a cell is sub-pixel to ~2 px.

## 2. Data format per LOD level

### 2.1 Node grid

- Level k node (k = 0..4, `mc.lod.maxLevel`): covers chunks `[nx 2^k, (nx + 1) 2^k) x [nz 2^k, ...)`, i.e. `16 * 2^k` blocks
  square, as 16 x H_k x 16 cells, H_k = world height >> k (overworld: 384, 192, 96, 48, 24), cell y origin `minY >> k`.
- Key: 64-bit `(k << 58) | (nx & 2^29-1) << 29 | (nz & 2^29-1)` (`LodManager.key`).
- Decoded (`LodData`): `int[] states` (global block-state ids, `Block.BLOCK_STATE_REGISTRY`), `byte[] light` (sky << 4 | block),
  biomes: level 0 = vanilla's 4x4x(H/4) quart grid (exact tints with vanilla's biome zoom and blend radius), level > 0 = one
  biome per column; biome keys as strings (registry ids differ between servers / datapacks).

### 2.2 Level 0 from a chunk (`LodData.fromCapture`)

Kept: block states, block light, sky light, biome quarts — everything `SectionCompiler` reads except block entities.
Two invisible-from-outside reductions (both measured on the encoded size and mesh size, results/STAGE3-R1.md):
1. **Dark air sealed**: air with sky light 0 becomes filler (stone) in dimensions with sky light. Caves, mineshafts, interiors
   of sealed buildings stop producing faces. A cave mouth keeps its lit air; where lit air meets sealed air a stone face is
   drawn (lit by the air in front, i.e. dark). Visible difference: looking through a window into a closed room at distance
   shows a dark stone face instead of the room (documented gap; under the spyglass at ~80 px per block it would show).
2. **Hidden blocks collapsed**: a full opaque block whose six neighbours inside the chunk are full opaque becomes filler
   (ores, stone variants, dirt under grass). Pixel-exact (the mesher culls those faces anyway; AO only tests opacity).
   Chunk-border columns are kept (their outside neighbour is unknown at capture).
- Light is stored only for cells that are not full opaque (the only light the mesher reads), so opaque runs merge.

### 2.3 Coarser levels (`LodData.downsample`)

Parent cell = the 2x2x2 child cells. Categories: air (also small non-full blocks: plants, torches, rails), fluid, solid-ish
(full opaque, leaves, snow layers mapped to snow blocks, filler). If at least 4 of 8 are solid-ish or fluid the cell takes the
most frequent such state of the upper child layer (else the lower; fluid only when fluids are the majority), so surfaces keep
their top material (grass over dirt) and the surface height rounds to the nearest cell; otherwise air with the maximum sky and
block light of the eight. Biome per column: the most frequent of the 2x2 child columns' surface biomes.
Error: geometric <= one cell (2^k blocks) per level step; thin features (single trunks, fences, plants) vanish at level >= 1,
where they are below ~2 px (tau). Measured Hausdorff error per node (ZOOM.md §3 `eps_n`) is [next]: M1 uses the texture rule
only (every coarse node counts as "averaged").

### 2.4 Encoded form

Deflate of: version, level, nx, nz, cell min y, height, biome key palette, biome indices, then per column (z-major) its
vertical runs `(state id varint, light byte, length varint)` bottom up. Measured sizes: results/STAGE3-R1.md §3. ZOOM.md's
target (3–5 KiB per chunk, 0.6–1.0 GiB for the 256-chunk disc) is the yardstick; the sealing makes level-0 nodes mostly
surface.

## 3. Mesh generation per level (`LodRegion`, `LodMesher`)

- **Mesher = vanilla's `SectionCompiler`** (our patched copy: S1 facing buckets, clusters) on a `RenderSectionRegion`
  subclass whose positions are cell coordinates of the node's level. Reads: the node and its 8 same-level neighbours; a
  missing neighbour repeats the nearest existing column (no walls at the edge of explored terrain); below the data is filler,
  above it air at full sky light. Light from the cached nibbles (`getBrightness` overridden); tints: level 0 runs
  `ClientLevel.calculateBlockTint`'s blend over a `BiomeManager` whose source is the cached quarts (vanilla's zoom seed), so
  level-0 tints equal vanilla's; level > 0 uses the column biome at the cell centre (blend radius below a cell is moot).
- Level 0 therefore is vanilla's own geometry of the cached blocks (block models, AO, smooth light, cross-plant offsets,
  cutout leaves per the options, fluids with their heights). This is ZOOM.md D2 — the property zoom needs.
- Coarser levels: the same mesher on the downsampled cells; vertex positions are multiplied by 2^k after meshing, so textures
  stretch over a cell (one tile per cell, mip-mapped): the "averaged" level of ZOOM.md §3, used only where a cell is <= tau px.
- Slabs: the node is cut into 16-cell slabs (vanilla's section shape); only slabs between the lowest open cell of the node and
  of its neighbours' adjacent edge columns and the top cell are meshed (cliff faces at node borders stay).
- Per slab and layer: 28-byte BLOCK vertices in facing-bucket order, 64-quad clusters with bounds in 1/16 cell (fits the
  kernel's 10-bit range at any level; the kernel scales them per section).
- Water / translucent: unsorted, through a translucent cluster pipeline (vanilla's translucent state). Overlapping translucent
  LOD surfaces (ice over water, stained glass) can blend in the wrong order: rare at distance; documented.
- Not in LOD meshes: block entities (chests, signs, beds, banners, skulls...), entities. ZOOM.md §10 [next]: block-entity
  impostors for the zoom range.

[next] Shader-mode mesher for stage 4 (OWN-SHADERS-RT §4.7) applies to LOD level 0 unchanged (same compiler path).

### 3.1 Round 1b: quad reduction (`LodGreedy`)

Measured on 9000 dumped slab-layers (`-Dmc.lod.dumpDir`, `tools/lod/greedy-tool.sh`, snowy taiga bench world): the vanilla
mesher's AO and smooth light make almost every face's corners differ (exact merging kept 80-90 % of the quads); with AO off at
levels >= 1 and loose keys (across random texture rotation and AO diagonal flips) merging keeps 57-61 % at levels 1-2 and pair
removal takes the inner faces of opaque canopies. Merging alone cannot give an order of magnitude on bumpy forest terrain
(singleton groups dominate); the order of magnitude comes from the selection (tau 16 instead of 2-8, level 0 only where a node
touches the real disc, levels up to 5) together with merging: 4.8 M -> 0.6 M drawn quads at LOD 64 (results/STAGE3-R1.md §R1b).

- Leaves: LOD meshes leaves opaque (`mc.lod.cutoutLeaves=false`, SOLID layer, like DH / Voxy far terrain); back-to-back
  full-cell opaque pairs are removed (exact for opaque cubes).
- Lighting: levels >= `mc.lod.aoLevel` (1) are meshed with AO off (flat light per face), so neighbouring faces merge exactly;
  level 0 keeps the player's smooth lighting and merges only within a colour tolerance of 4.
- Merged faces repeat their texture once per cell through the round-12 tile wrap (`TERRAIN_GREEDY`), drawn by the LOD greedy
  cluster pipelines; the per-block random rotation pattern is lost inside a merged face.
- Coarse snow: a snow cover over leaves keeps the leaves (otherwise level-1 canopies turn into white snow cubes).

### 3.2 Round 4: tree stand-ins (`LodTrees`)

The round-3 diagnosis: at the default detail the far forest is 2- and 4-block cubes (levels 1-2) where vanilla shows 1-block
spruce rings; that was the largest perceptual error. Stand-ins replace a tree's cells by one textured quad:
- Records (3 ints per tree; CONTRACT 16.S3-LOD-R4) detected on level-0 data when level 1 is downsampled, concatenated up to
  `mc.lod.treeMax` (2), recorded by the seed generator for generated nodes, carried in the node encoding (LodData v2).
- Mesh time (levels 1-2): the trees' leaves / snow covers / trunk cells mesh as air (crown cylinders from the 3x3 nodes' lists),
  and each tree adds one CUTOUT quad: a cylindrical billboard (rotated toward the camera in the compact vertex function) with an
  impostor sprite (orthographic view of a vanilla-shaped spruce / pine / broadleaf crown at 20 deg elevation, AO and leaf shade
  calibrated so the far forest's mean colour matches real RD 32 within 1-2 levels of luminance).
- Cost: one quad per tree instead of ~5 cube faces at level 1, but alpha-tested fragments; the opaque visible quads at LOD 256 drop
  (tau 16: 78 k -> 62-68 k, tau 8: 226 k -> 195-219 k) while the FPS ratio stays (results/STAGE3-R4.md).
- Limits: side view only (from steep angles the crowns look narrow), one canonical shape per kind (heights / widths scaled),
  resource-pack leaf textures are not followed, level 0 keeps vanilla trees (zoom needs them).

### 3.3 Round 5: the wave far field (`LodWave`, native/src/mcmetal_lodwave.m)

Why: Voxy's selection (screenspace.glsl: subdivide while a 32-voxel section's projected area exceeds `subDivisionSize^2` = 64^2
px) keeps voxels at about 1-2 px, i.e. our tau 2. Rasterizing that is ~16x the quads of tau 8 (48 M drawn quads, 3.1 GB of
meshes, 178 FPS at 512 chunks, results/STAGE3-R5.md): the raster LOD is bound by tiny triangles. A far field whose cost scales
with pixels and cells along view lines instead of faces:
- Data: per node 256 columns of runs (lo, hi, material) top down, hidden runs merged (the store's own column-run form); per
  column a top (ushort) for the march and a word (run offset, count, biome) for shading. ~3.5 KiB per node, 157-241 MB for the
  512-chunk disc at tau 2 (meshes: 3.1 GB).
- Visibility: with no camera roll every vertical world plane through the eye is one screen line through the vertical vanishing
  point. Front-to-back along such a plane, a column is visible exactly where its top's elevation slope exceeds every nearer
  column's ("wave surfing", Voxlap / Comanche). On the GPU one SIMD group per line, its 32 lanes = 32 distance segments: pass 1
  maxima, SIMD prefix max = occlusion from nearer segments, pass 2 writes span markers, a fill pass turns them into per-row column
  ids. Cost ~ lines x cells per line (~4-9 k lines, ~3-4 k cells per line at tau 2): 2.3 ms at 3024x1964.
- Panorama: the per-plane result depends on the eye position only, not on yaw / pitch. The planes are sampled by world
  direction (N = 2 pi K 1.15) and rows become elevation-angle bins; a standing eye builds it once (~4-6 ms) and then only runs
  the composite (0.14 ms over the whole far field at the spin benchmark), whatever the camera turns; a moving eye rebuilds one of
  6 slices per frame (0.85 ms, at most 6 frames of parallax lag). Zoomed (K >= 3000) uses per-frame screen lines (2.4 ms).
- Composite: per pixel its line and row -> column id -> the pixel's own ray against that face (exact block edges at any
  distance), material at the hit height, atlas texel (block-repeating, mip by footprint), biome tint, vanilla face shade,
  sky light 15, vanilla terrain fog with the LOD compression, its own depth (clouds / translucents / entities test against it).
- Hybrid ring (`mc.lod.wave.ringPx`, default 0; 4 = quality option): where blocks are larger than 4 px (192 to ~350 blocks
  unzoomed) level 0 stays raster meshes from the vanilla mesher (AO, smooth light, plants, cutout shapes): the wave's
  simplifications are visible at 3-7 px per block (perceptual check at R 32: wave alone vista SSIM 0.834, with the ring 0.922),
  but the ring costs 1011 -> 754 FPS at the spin benchmark and halves travel FPS (re-meshing as it moves): off by default.
- Limits (r5): beyond the ring columns are solid from the top (gaps under canopies shaded; a per-pixel march through them exists
  but costs ~0.5 ms), opaque water, no AO / smooth light, no plants, no roll, no far field while the vanishing point is on
  screen (looking steeply down or up), zoom uses the per-frame lines (the panorama is limited to K < 3000).

## 4. Storage and disk cache (`LodStore`)

- Key: `<game dir>/lod/sp/<save folder>/<dimension>/` in singleplayer, `<game dir>/lod/mp/<server address>/<dimension>/` in
  multiplayer. [next] world fingerprint (seed hash in singleplayer; for servers a hash of a few spawn-area chunks) to drop a
  cache after a map reset; per-entry "last seen" for LRU deletion with a disk cap (ZOOM.md: default 16 GB proposed).
- Region files of 32 x 32 nodes per level: `L<k>/r.<rx>.<rz>.lod` (magic, level, rx, rz, 1024 lengths, blobs), written whole via
  temp file + atomic rename: periodically (15 s), before eviction from memory, and on close. Crash = at most 15 s of captures lost
  (they are re-captured / re-read).
- Memory: LRU of regions bounded by `mc.lod.ramMB` (default 384). Decoded nodes: a 20-entry cache per worker thread.
- Never written to the save, never visible to a server.

## 5. Selection, culling and drawing

### 5.1 Selection (scheduler thread, ~20 Hz, `LodManager.select`)

- Quadtree from level-4 roots over the disc of `mc.lod.radius`. Per node: d = horizontal distance from the camera to the node
  box; K = this frame's focal length in panel px (`height / 2 * |P[1][1]|`, zoom included) if the node box is inside the
  frame frustum, else the unzoomed option-FOV value (ZOOM.md §3 residency R1/R3: zooming refines only the cone).
- Wanted level = `floor(log2(tau d / K))` clamped to [0, maxLevel] (r1b: 5). Split while wanted < level; nodes holding a chunk
  inside vanilla's round view distance always split to level 0 (r1b; M1 used the RD + 1 square, which put ~200 full-detail
  nodes in the square's corners).
- r1b GPU-budget control: the selection's bytes (resident sizes, per-level averages for pending nodes) above 75 % of
  `mc.lod.gpuMB` raise tau by 1.25x (at most 16x), below 45 % lower it slowly: a too-fine tau (or the spyglass) coarsens the
  selection instead of ending in refused uploads and evict / re-mesh churn.
- Coarse until fine (ZOOM.md H1): a split node whose subtree is not complete (meshes pending) is drawn itself if resident,
  never across the real area.
- Meshing priority: distance (in-view first). Re-mesh when the node's or a neighbour's data version changes, once the 3x3 has
  been quiet for 1 s (bulk ingest would otherwise re-mesh a node up to 9 times).
- [next] measured geometric error per node, hysteresis (x2) against FOV wobble, and the dithered cross-fade (H3) for late
  refinements; prefetch on spyglass-in-hand (P1) and the permanent 50x cone (P3).

### 5.2 GPU path (`LodGpu`, render thread)

- Arenas: 256 MB Shared Metal buffers (unified memory: meshes are copied straight into them), first-fit in whole quads, frees
  deferred 6 frames. Budget `mc.lod.gpuMB` (default 1536); over 90 % the least recently selected unselected nodes are evicted.
- One section slot per meshed slab: a persistent section info (origin, visibility 1) and a persistent cull-table entry
  `(origin - C0, unit) (C0 - origin, 0)` relative to a base point C0 (re-based beyond 4096 blocks).
- Kernel (native/src/mcmetal_clusters.m, round 12's, extended): flag 4 = persistent table; the view block carries
  `camDelta = camera - C0` and `eyeDelta = eye - C0`; per-section `unit` scales the cluster bounds (2^k / 16 blocks).
  Exactness of the cull is round 12's (frustum + facing on conservative boxes).
- Cluster items of the drawn set are rebuilt only when the selection, residency or loaded chunks change (6-buffer ring); per
  frame the CPU writes one 128-byte view and the jobs (one per layer and arena) — no per-frame section table, no per-cluster
  CPU work.
- Draw order: SOLID and CUTOUT LOD after the layer's real terrain (real terrain is nearer: its depth rejects hidden LOD
  fragments), TRANSLUCENT LOD before the real translucent sections (farther first). Same pipelines' fragment stage as the real
  terrain (fog, lightmap, atlas, RGSS / anisotropy), so the look matches vanilla's terrain by construction.
- [next] HiZ for LOD (ZOOM.md §4 item 2), compact vertex format, merged coarse quads, a write kernel with one thread per
  cluster (the round-12 write kernel launches 64 threads per cluster, cheap for 20k clusters, wasteful for 200k+).

### 5.3 Seams with the real chunks (RD 12–16)

- A level-0 node is dropped exactly when vanilla renders that chunk: inside `ChunkTrackingView.isInViewDistance` of the camera
  chunk and loaded in the client (multiplayer servers with a smaller view distance leave the LOD visible there).
- Same mesher, same neighbour data on both sides of the edge, so the edge faces match (faces between a real chunk and a LOD
  chunk are culled on both sides).
- Known seam gaps (M1): vanilla's section fade-in mixes new real sections toward the fog colour over 0.75 s although the LOD
  already showed them (ZOOM.md H5: skip the fade where level 0 covered); a newly loaded chunk shows nothing until its sections
  compile (~ms, but visible as a one-frame hole while flying); coarse-level neighbours of different levels can leave cracks of
  up to a cell where a surface steps (skirts [next]).

### 5.4 Fog, sky and far plane

- With LOD drawing, `GameRenderer.extractCamera` passes `max(RD, LOD radius)` as vanilla's render distance to `FogRenderer`
  (render-distance fog start/end and sky fog end move out) and `Camera.update` raises `depthFar` to cover the radius. The
  result is vanilla's image at RD = LOD radius: the fog no longer hides the real-chunk edge, and the LOD edge gets vanilla's
  fog. 26.3 renders reversed-Z into a float depth buffer (`Projection.getMatrix` swaps near / far; depth test GEQUAL), so the
  far plane at 7,700 blocks costs no precision.
- Clouds (cloud range 128) are depth-tested against the LOD like real terrain.
- Environmental fog (overworld attributes, rain, water, blindness...) is untouched.
- **[r2] LOD fog** (results/STAGE3-R2.md §R2.1): vanilla's overworld environmental fog is solid at 1024 blocks, which hid 3/4
  of the 256-chunk disc. Terrain shaders now feed the environmental fog the compressed distance d - max(d - R0, 0) c (R0 = real
  render distance; the LOD edge maps to 320 blocks of vanilla haze), the render-distance ramp covers the last 20 % of the LOD
  radius (fade into the horizon colour), and LOD draws are skipped while the environmental fog ends before R0 (nether, water).

## 6. Ingestion (sources)

| Source | When | Thread | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| Client chunks (singleplayer and multiplayer) | 1 s after `ClientLevel.onChunkLoaded` once `lightOnInColumn`; again at `ClientLevel.unload` (final state) | render thread copies (palette containers, light nibbles, biome keys: ~tens of µs), workers encode | M1 |
| Integrated server's saved chunks (singleplayer) | rings around the player out to the radius, re-scanned when the player moves 8 chunks | `chunkMap.read` = the server's own IO worker (thread-safe, read-only), parse + encode on LOD workers | M1 |
| Seed-based generation (singleplayer: world seed; multiplayer: user-supplied seed) | selected nodes without data (r3) | LOD workers, nearest first | §6.1: LodGen at the node's own level into the generated store; headless test vs vanilla's base height (LodSelfTest) |
| Multiplayer beyond what was received | never (no request exists; GOAL multiplayer rule) | — | unexplored directions stay sky / fog |

Nothing is sent, nothing in the protocol changes, nothing is written to the save. Fabric: no vanilla signature changed;
hooks are single static calls (`Lod.*`) inside `Camera.update`, `GameRenderer.extractCamera`, `ClientLevel.onChunkLoaded` /
`unload`, `LevelRenderer.prepareChunkRendersIndirectFast`, `ChunkSectionsToRender.DrawIndirect`, plus a registered pipeline
(`translucent_terrain_cluster`, only with `-Dmc.lod=true`).

### 6.1 Seed-based generation (round 3, `LodGen`)

- When a selected node has no real data (none captured, none saved, nothing below it to downsample), a worker generates it at
  its own level from the seed: ~7-14 ms per node at any level (results/STAGE3-R3.md), so a level-5 node (1024 chunks) costs
  as much as a level-1 node and a 1000-chunk disc fills in seconds.
- Terrain: `overworld/sloped_cheese` (final density without caves / noodles / ore veins / cave entrances) plus final density's
  top and bottom gradients, sampled on vanilla's own interpolation lattice (4 x 8 x 4; at levels >= 3 one sample per cell
  corner horizontally, 8 blocks vertically) and interpolated as vanilla interpolates final density, so the surface is vanilla's
  except where caves open at the surface. Other dimensions / datapacks without that function: their final density.
- Surface: vanilla's surface rules per column (`LodMaterial` evaluates them for one column with vanilla's context: surface depth
  noise, gradients from the neighbour cells, stone depth, water height) for the top cell and the cells exposed on slopes;
  deeper cells are sealed filler. Sea-level water; ice / snow / snowy leaves where the biome is too cold to rain (vanilla's
  freeze_top_layer). Trees: per chunk a deterministic set by biome (counts and shapes after vanilla's tree features), voxelized
  into cell counts; a cell becomes leaves at 1 block (level 0), 4 of 8 (level 1, the real downsample's rule) or
  `mc.lod.genFill` of its volume (levels >= 2).
- Round 4: generated nodes carry tree records (stand-ins at levels 1-2, LodTrees), and at levels >= 3 a ground cell whose column
  holds canopy over half its area takes the leaves (`mc.lod.genCanopy` 0.5), as the real downsample keeps a forest's canopy on
  the ground cell.
- Store: `<store>/gen-<seed>-<version>/`, same region format, disk cap `mc.lod.genDiskMB` (farthest regions deleted first; a
  deleted node is generated again when selected). Real data always wins (`LodManager.load`), and downsamples fill missing
  quadrants from the generated node of their level, so a coarse node is never half empty where real chunks end.
- Sources of the seed: singleplayer = the integrated server's generator (datapacks included); multiplayer = only a seed the
  user gives (`-Dmc.lod.seed`, `lod/seeds.properties` per server address) with vanilla's overworld generator. Nothing is sent to
  or requested from a server.

| GPU meshes | <= 1536 MB (`mc.lod.gpuMB`), 112 B per quad | compact 8–16 B per quad; 256-chunk disc within ~500 MB |

| Render thread | capture copies (8 chunks / frame max), uploads (<= 48 MB / frame, memcpy into Shared arenas), item rebuild on change, ~200 B per frame | same |

## 9. Compatibility with stage 4 / 5

- One node pyramid, one mesher path: stage 4's shader-mode mesher (OWN-SHADERS-RT §4.7) applies to LOD level 0 as to real
  sections; LOD draws are cluster draws (§4.8, §6.14), so G-buffer variants of the cluster pipelines cover them.
- The kernel's flag-4 path takes a view per call: shadow cascades and C4 are more calls over the same persistent lists
  (§10.3 "one SSE kernel").
- The node data is the source of the far ray fields: level-0 nodes give the mid-field occupancy (§5.4), coarse nodes the column
  runs (the encoded form already is per-column runs), with tmin >= the node's error.
